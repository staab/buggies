# Phase 1 - terrain

- Every map is an island, with one or two mountain peaks in the center.
- Mountains become foothills before they become plains.
- Each map should have 1-2 rivers which begin in the mountains and follow the terrain to the sea, creating lakes if necessary in order to escape low spots, before running over as rivers again.

# Phase 2 - districts

- Demarcate cities, suburbs, and country.
- Cities and suburbs must be located in level areas away from significant elevation changes.

# Phase 3 - roads

- First, create a highway system that connects all cities and loops - it must have no dead ends. If it has to cross a river, use a bridge. If it has to cross a mountain, use a tunnel. Elevation may change, but slowly. Curves should be gradual.
- Next, create periodic exits and interchanges (only in plains) - standard on/off ramps, with an underpass connecting both sides.
- From these off ramps, draw arterials - these may loop, intersect, or dead-end. Arterials may cross rivers on bridges, but cannot tunnel through mountains. They may go halfway up mountains, but should never be very steep - use switchbacks or a gradual line to climb and descend.
- Finally, in cities draw a grid between the arterials of city streets.
- Highways should tunnel through mountains, arterials should wind their way up the mountains (not go straight up).

# Phase 4 - furniture

- In cities, fill the spaces between the city streets with buildings varying in height.
- In suburbs, add houses along all arterials.
- In the country, add trees and occasional houses next to the arterials.
